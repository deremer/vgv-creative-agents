# Lateral Prompts

A deck for breaking stuck thinking. Pull at random. Apply it to whatever you're working on. Don't argue with the card. Go wide, broad, and deep to challenge your thinking and unlock areas of unexpected creativity.

## How to use it

Pull a card. Think about it to consider how it applies. If you have to force it to make it apply, discard and draw another. The card is right more often than you think. They are meant to create "lateral thinking" where an unexpected line of reasoning can yieled new insights, ideas, and directionsof exploration. If you reject three in a row, the problem is you, not the deck. Don't draw again until you've tried the first one.

---

## Categories

The deck mixes seven kinds of cards. You don't need to know which is which when you draw. Knowing helps when you're writing new ones.

**Strategy.** Imperative moves on the work itself. *Subtract until it hurts. Reverse the flow. Build the smallest version.* These tell you what to do.

**Gnomic.** A single word or fragment. *Saturation. Hinge. Husk.* You have to interpret. The interpretation is the work.

**Permission.** Lets the maker off a hook they put themselves on. *It's allowed to be small. Stop apologizing for it. Nothing today is fine.* These don't push, they release.

**Body.** Engages the maker as a physical creature. *Stand up. Where is the tension in your jaw. Eat first.* The body knows things the spreadsheet doesn't.

**Maker psychology.** Interrogates the person, not the artifact. *Whose voice are you using. What are you proving. The fear is information.* The work is fine. You're the variable.

**Tactical.** A specific studio move. *Use only the rejected pieces. Restart with the third draft. Build it for one specific person.* Concrete, executable, weird.

**Koan.** Resists easy interpretation on purpose. *A door is also a wall. The slow path arrives first. What the work knows.* You stare at it. Sometimes nothing happens. Sometimes everything does.

## Guidelines for writing new cards

1. **Imperative or fragment, never explanation.** A card is a verb or a noun. Not a paragraph.
2. **Under nine words.** Most under five. If you can't compress it, you don't have a card yet.
3. **No hedges.** Cut "maybe," "consider," "try to," "might want to." The card is wrong sometimes. That's fine.
4. **One move per card.** If it does two things, it's two cards or it's mush.
5. **Test against the deck.** If the lateral pull is the same as a card already in, kill one. Cards must differ in what they make you do, not in how they're worded.
6. **Strangeness counts.** A card the user has heard before does no work. The deck earns its keep when the card surprises.
7. **No CEO voice.** "Align stakeholders." "Drive impact." Delete on sight. The deck is for the studio, not the boardroom.
8. **Read it at 2am.** If the card sounds like a LinkedIn post, it's wrong. If it sounds like a friend who knows the work, it's right.
9. **Permission cards are not pep talks.** "You can do it" is a pep talk. "Nothing today is fine" is permission. The difference is the second one tells the truth.
10. **Don't explain the koan.** If the card needs a footnote, it's not a koan, it's a footnote.
